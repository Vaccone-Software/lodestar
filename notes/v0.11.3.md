Eight cards, and not one of them explains when to reach for a tool.

A definition and the way in is the whole card now. Your apps, found by name.
Letters that lead to apps. A saved arrangement of windows. A history of
everything you copy. The line telling somebody when a tool was the right one
was doing nothing the definition had not already done, and it arrived in the
same place on every card, which is how a deck starts reading as a form.

While the walkthrough is up the keyboard is entirely its own. A gesture used to
reach the engine from under the card, so lode space summoned a window behind
the walkthrough and escape then closed that window instead of the card being
read; holding lode alone laid the graph guide over the top. Nothing does that
now. Only the combinations that belong to macOS still pass.

Nothing leaves the walkthrough either. Where a sentence cannot carry a tool the
card draws it, at the real geometry and from real data: the clipboard card lays
out the pin column and the whole home row exactly where they sit on screen, and
the graph card shows your own addresses with a branch in them if you have one,
so the thing that makes a graph a graph rather than a list of letters is on the
page.

The web bar is called Ask. It takes a saved name, a bare domain or a question,
your routes decide which browser profile the answer opens in, and making
Lodestar your default browser puts every link clicked in any app through those
same routes.

Accessibility asks for the permission rather than describing where it lives:
`space` shows the macOS prompt, and once macOS has shown it that key opens the
pane instead, because it only ever prompts once.

Keys that are not the way in sit on the card beside, under a heading where one
card teaches two tools, so `⇧label` can never be read against scroll mode. Each
key wears its own cap: `j` `k` `h` `l` in one cap read as four keys to type in
order.

Two things the drawings caught, both older than this release. There are five pin
slots, and the reference has said three since the day they became five. And the
starter graph proposes five addresses onto a card that was showing four of them,
so accepting it wrote a letter nobody had been shown.

The closing note is gone until it is written, and the walkthrough no longer asks
your name: a required field in front of a stranger, for a coach that does not
exist yet.
