The draft settles into its shape.

It is set in the system's mono face now. A block cursor in a
proportional face is a new width on every character, and the eye pays
for that on every glance; in mono the cursor is one constant block,
and the find lights keep their weight without moving a single letter,
because a mono face's advances survive the weight by design. The
lights also go out the moment you act. After the jump, the cursor is
the answer, and nothing lingers behind it.

`j` and `k` walk the lines you see. A wrapped line is one line to the
file and several to the eye, and the draft now sides with the eye,
asking the very layout that drew the wrap where one line down lands.
An operator's `j` stays logical, so `dj` keeps meaning what vim means
by it.

And the ghost holds its place. Words still dimmed when you start
typing become text on the spot, reserved where they stand; your typed
characters land after them, and the recognizer's final revises the
reserved words in place when it arrives. Dictate, type, dictate — it
lands in the order it happened, instead of the interjection elbowing
ahead of the sentence it interrupted.
