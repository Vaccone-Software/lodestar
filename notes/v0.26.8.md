It comes back.

0.26.7 crashed on `j`. The new visual-line seam read the draft's buffer
while the editor already held it exclusively, and Swift aborts a
process for that, every time, deterministically. The seam now hands the
buffer through by value, and the crash is pinned by tests at three
depths: the exact scenario through the real panel, every key, operator,
find target, and text object thrown at the editor in bulk, and seeded
storms of thousands of keys through both the pure machine and the real
draft. A motion is never allowed to be a gamble again.

Worse than the crash was the silence after it. The login agent has
always known how to resurrect a dead lodestar — but an instance started
by the updater, Finder, or a rollback runs unsupervised, so every
self-update quietly traded that away. Now a lodestar that finds the
agent idle hands its session back to launchd and bows out; the resident
process is always the one that comes back on its own, within seconds.

And a crash leaves less behind. The next boot knows the previous run
died rather than quit and says so in the log, uncaught exceptions are
written down before the fall, and a draft in flight is stashed half a
second behind your hands: whatever you had said and typed is waiting on
the pasteboard when lodestar returns.
