A video in the browser now steps aside while the draft listens. On a Bluetooth headset, opening the microphone drops the sound to telephone quality, so the draft has paused Music and Spotify since August and picked them up again afterward. It could not do the same for YouTube, because a browser cannot be asked what it is playing. A browser does say which tabs are making sound, in the label behind the speaker icon on the tab, so the draft now reads that and presses the keyboard's play and pause key. When the draft ends it presses the key again, once the headset is back to full quality. If you started the video yourself in the meantime, it leaves the video alone.

That key is a toggle, and it goes to whichever app last held the media controls, so every press is checked about four seconds later. If the press started Music, Music is paused again. If it started a video you had paused just before the draft, because the speaker icon stays up for about three seconds after the sound stops, a second press stops it. So you may hear up to four seconds of sound in that case, and in neither case does anything restart when the draft ends.

This works in Brave and Chrome when the browser's language is English. It was measured on Brave, and other browsers are left alone, as they were before. As with Music, it only happens when the headset is both your microphone and your output. On laptop speakers or wired headphones the sound does not degrade, so nothing is paused.
