Lodestar begins with the thing you came for.

The first launch opens on a welcome with four doors: Write, Switch, Keep
and Speak, the same four the website shows. Choose the one you want
first, and Lodestar walks you through that one to a real result: a typo
fixed where you wrote it, an app reached by the launcher and then by a
letter of its own, a copy pasted back from the strip, a sentence spoken
into place. The other three wait, and each is brought up once, later,
when it would help.

Only one permission is asked for at the start, Accessibility, and the
card says what your door needs it for before macOS asks. On an account
that is not an administrator, the card says so up front, because turning
it on needs an administrator's password, and it can copy a short note
for your IT team.

Writing starts on spelling, with nothing to download. Once the first fix
is taken, Lodestar offers the closest model your Mac can run, and says
what it costs before anything downloads.

Switching teaches the launcher before any letters, because on the first
day the launcher already works: type a few letters of an app and it
opens filling the screen. Letters come after, suggested from the apps
you have open, and kept only if you keep them.

Nothing changes for anyone already using Lodestar.
