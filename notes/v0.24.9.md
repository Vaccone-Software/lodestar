A clicked link now brings the browser with it, unless you were in the
middle of something.

Lodestar parks what it is not showing. That is what makes the screen
quiet, and it is also what made a clicked link feel broken. The link
opened, the browser came forward, and the window it opened in was a one
pixel sliver in the corner of a display you were not looking at. Nothing
you could see had happened. Thirteen days of this machine's own records
say the browser was parked for 83% of clicked links, and that half of
those were followed, a couple of seconds later, by going and fetching
the browser by hand.

The rule had been that a link someone sent you is not a request to
rearrange your layout. That was right about arrangements and wrong about
everything else, because it was written as though doing nothing left the
browser where it was. It does not. Parking is Lodestar's own doing, so
declining to undo it is not neutrality — it is the one outcome that
cannot happen on a Mac without Lodestar running.

So the rule keeps what it was protecting and drops what it was not. **A
link brings the browser in when there is nothing to disturb, and never
touches an arrangement you built.** One window on a display is not an
arrangement. It is the display showing one thing, and replacing it is
what your hand was going to do anyway. Two windows is something you
made, and a link does not get to take it apart.

When it holds back, it says so, because the whole difficulty with the
protected case is that the screen deliberately does not move — and a
screen that does not move looks exactly like a click that did nothing.
A chip names where the link went and stands for a minute. Tap lode
twice and you are there. It goes quiet the moment the screen moves by
any other road, since its one claim is that nothing moved, and it holds
the coach off while it stands so that two voices never ask for the same
two taps.

A link that matched one of your rules was quietly worse off than one
that did not. Routing to a browser profile goes out through a second
Chromium that hands the URL to the first and exits, and the right to
come forward died with the process that forwarded it — so the rule you
wrote to send a link somewhere specific made it _less_ likely to arrive
in front of you. It asks now, like every other link.

And keys are drawn as keys. A gesture spelled `lode lode` was one cap
with a word inside it that happened to be written twice. It is two caps
now, because it is two presses, and the chips and the guide had drifted
into saying that differently. One cap is one press.
