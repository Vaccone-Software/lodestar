The lode key can be tapped.

It was a hold: down, a letter, up, and the lens stood while it was
down. Four days of the raw record said what that hold was for. Ninety-
four in a hundred covered exactly one key, and a hold that buys one key
is a co-contraction bought for nothing — the thumb pressing against the
letter for two hundred milliseconds, a hundred and ninety times a day.

So a tap arms the next key instead. Lode down and up with nothing
struck inside it, shorter than a peek, and the key that follows within
a second is the gesture it would have been under the hold, routed the
same way, the phrase ending with the key the way it ends when the thumb
lifts. The hold is unchanged and is still how the map appears, which is
what gives the two forms their meaning: tap for the gesture the hand
owns, hold to see the map. The pill waits a third of a second before it
says lode is armed, because nine gestures in ten strike inside that and
a pill on every one would be motion; past it, the pill says so, and
leaves with the key. Escape disarms. A tap followed by nothing expires
silently. Tapping twice is still assent to the coach and never leaves a
key armed behind it. And the record showed the cost as well as the
gain — a short empty press followed by a letter now becomes a gesture
rather than that letter, fourteen times in four days — so `lode.tap` is
a switch, on by default, and every press that arrives armed is marked
in the raw record, where the trade can be read rather than argued.

And the record knows where your keys are.

It kept a finger per press by reading the keycode the way a typing
course reads a Mac keyboard. On a split board that puts enter,
backspace, space and half the modifiers under the thumbs, every one of
those presses was charged to the pinky, and one lode press — three
modifier records — was scattered across three digits. The convention
was wrong on exactly the boards whose owners care.

Settings has its first page behind a pane for it. Coach → Health →
Keyboards opens to one board, chosen at the top, its fourteen keys
below with the standard placement labelled as such, and a placement
that differs is the only thing ever written: `health.keyboards`, per
keyboard and per key, "right thumb". Letters keep their columns on
every board that keeps columns and are never on the page. The tap
writes the finger under that declaration, and every past row can be
read under it again, because the record kept a kind, a modifier bit
and a side. The hand column is never touched — it is the split the
published index was defined on, and the sides and the pairs come out
identical under any map. A changed map is a new era in the ring.

The Health row once called Handedness is now Dominant hand.
