Your health record can now be read for analysis. `lodestar health` prints it as JSON lines, one row each, at the grain you ask for: a summary over any dates, days, weeks or months, or the record's own events (quarter hour pulses, 90 second windows, bouts of typing, blind spans, dictation and clicks). Nothing is written to disk, and what you do with the lines is yours, whether that is a script, a local model or Claude. The keystroke rows are held back unless you add `--include-presses`, because hand, finger and the spaces between words come close to a transcript of what you typed. `--since` and `--until` choose the days.

Older data comes out whole and says how it was measured. Every row names the clock that timed a hold and what a keyboard list meant at the time, and a period is split only where a number's meaning changed (at 0.34.0 and 0.39.2), so no row averages across that change. A measure Lodestar was not yet recording is written as null, never as zero. `lodestar health schema` explains every field.

Behind the scenes, the test suite no longer takes your keyboard focus while it runs, and it can run in a virtual Mac of its own so it never needs your screen at all.
