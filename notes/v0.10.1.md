Lodestar starts paying attention to how you reach things.

Not to report anywhere. To notice, on this machine, so that later it can
tell you something worth acting on: an address you bound and never type,
one you abandon halfway through, one that never gets faster no matter how
often you use it, an app you keep searching for that has no address at
all. Those four situations have four different answers, and until now
there was no way for Lodestar to know it was looking at one.

What it records is counts, never content. That an address was typed. The
pauses inside it. Whether a chain was escaped out of. Which road reached
an app, and how many characters a search cost before you picked
something. It does not record a window title, a URL, a host, a clipboard,
or the letters you typed into the searcher. The count is enough to know
what a search cost; the letters are yours. The file sits in
~/.local/share, deliberately not in the config, which is the directory
people commit to a dotfiles repository, and nothing ever leaves the
machine.

Two measurements carry most of it, and neither has to guess at what you
would have done otherwise. Pauses: an address you own is typed at motor
speed, and one you are reconstructing has a gap in it, which is about as
close to reading muscle memory as software can get without asking. And
abandons: a chain you started and gave up on is unambiguous in a way a
slow chain is not, because a slow chain might be a phone call. Anything
over ten seconds is discarded rather than averaged, since chains wait
indefinitely by design and one interruption would otherwise make every
number here a lie.

lodestar observations prints all of it plainly, because a store you
cannot read is one you cannot consent to, and lodestar observations clear
deletes it. observations.enabled false means nothing is recorded and no
file is written.

The report stays quiet where the counts are thin, which is most of a
first week. A finding built on absence needs time to have passed before
it means anything, since an address you have not used today says nothing
at all. A finding built on presence only needs its counts. Silence is
cheap, and one confident recommendation drawn from three data points
would spend exactly the trust that makes an opinionated tool worth
using.

No recommendations yet. They arrive once the data has had a chance to say
whether they would have been any good.
