The pulse learns shape.

A mean and a standard deviation describe a bell and nothing else, and
the interesting part of a motor measurement is in its tail. The health
pulse keeps shape now, under the line it has always been under: what
that line forbids is key identity, and a shape is not an identity.

Hold time goes in beside the counts, press to release. It is the one
keyboard measurement with a clinical literature behind it, and it asks
for nothing the line forbids, because how long a press lasted is not
which key it was. A press the system repeated is never timed, for the
same reason its keydowns were never counted.

The pauses stop being thrown away. A gap past the motor ceiling used to
be dropped so that thinking time could not contaminate a motor number,
and now it is kept beside it instead. The old numbers mean exactly what
they always meant. The tail is simply no longer lost.

Bouts are the unit the hands actually work in. A bout ends when the
hands stop for longer than ten minutes, and every pulse now records
where in its bout it sat. Whether the hands drift as a bout runs on is
worked out when you read it, each bout measured against itself first,
so a long day cannot pass itself off as a slow hour. The answer comes
with its own error, and where the error swallows the effect the
printout says flat rather than dressing up nothing as something.

The day's shape is read with the measures the sleep literature settled
on for exactly this kind of record, and the day begins at four in the
morning, or an evening that runs past midnight is cut in half and
counted as two.

`lodestar observations` shows all of it. None of it is judged: a rhythm
is described, and what you want your rhythm to be is yours.
