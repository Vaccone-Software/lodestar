Typing no longer waits on the editor.

Every key you press passes through Lodestar on its way to the app in
front, and since the editor arrived it had been waiting there. On each
keystroke in a field the editor reads, it worked out its marks on the
same thread that passes the keys along: a few hundredths of a second in
a chat message, a quarter of a second in a long email, a full second at
the editor's limit, which is long enough for macOS to stop sending keys
through at all. That work now happens to one side, and the editor asks
what language a sentence is in once rather than on every key. The keys
go straight through.

The editor's model also stays ready for longer. It was let go two
minutes after the last sentence, so coming back from a short pause
meant waiting several seconds while it loaded again, twenty gigabytes
at a time for the larger model. It is now kept for twenty minutes, and
still let go at once when macOS says memory is short. A small leak that
grew with every load is closed.

A pick made with a capital no longer holds shift down for the click.
Click hints and select are chosen with a capital letter, and the click
that followed carried the shift, which Brave reads as open in a new
window. Nothing Lodestar clicks, drags, scrolls or types now carries a
key your hand is holding.

Keeping a word in the editor no longer quits Lodestar, and neither does
copying a line like hsl(nan 50% 50%). A slow start after a crash is no
longer mistaken for a freeze, which had turned one crash into several,
and a few waits on other programs that could freeze the app now happen
off to the side.

Lodestar also notices keyboards that connect after it starts. Its list
of keyboards had been fixed at launch, so a Bluetooth keyboard that
reconnected later never appeared, on the Keyboards page or in the
record, and its keys were counted as the laptop's. Each key is now
counted to the keyboard it came from, and a keyboard with mouse keys is
no longer taken for a mouse.
