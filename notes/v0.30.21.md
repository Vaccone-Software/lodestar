The mirror learns to price the pointer, dictation, and what was left
undone.

The mouse now measures its own reach, not just counts it. From the tap's
motion the instrument reads the parts a click has always been assumed to
cost: the homing off the keys, the travel, the settle onto the target,
the press, and the trip back to the keys, with the path the pointer drew
against the straight line it needed. Positions never leave the tap's
thread; only seconds and distance do. The overhead's pointing line reads
as measured once most of a window's clicks were timed, and the older
trip flag gives way to the reach that began after a keystroke, read from
motion instead of inferred from order.

Dictation joins the scorecard it was missing from. Every spoken draft's
open time is priced against the words it kept at a natural speaking rate,
so a draft that landed nothing is dictation's overhead the way the
backspace loop is typing's. Beside the pointing pool stands a new
abandonment view: each surface's share of openings that ended in
nothing, and the seconds they cost, gathered from the giving-up every
surface already logged and summed at last in one place. The draft's
fixed cost is split by whether the speech model was warm, so the value
of keeping it warm is a number rather than a guess.

A held key is no longer read as typing. Autorepeat keydowns, which the
system emits every thirty milliseconds while a key is down, now mark the
minute present and count as neither keystrokes nor rhythm, so an evening
with a key held reads as presence and not as a hand typing faster than
any hand can.
