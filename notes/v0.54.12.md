The draft can be pointed at. Until now its words answered only the keys, so fixing the third word of a sentence meant walking the cursor back to it. Now a click puts the caret where you clicked, on the nearer side of the letter, and a drag selects. A double click takes a word, a triple click takes a line, and dragging after either grows the selection by whole words or whole lines. Shift and a click extends whatever is selected. A click in the margin beside a line counts too, the way it does in any Mac field.

In the Vim style the mouse is vim's own. A click puts the cursor on the letter and drops a command you had half typed, and a drag, a double click or a triple click is visual mode, so what you dragged is what d, c, y and your voice act on next. Speak over a dragged selection and your words take its place.

None of it takes the draft away from the app underneath. A click never makes the draft the active window, so that app keeps its cursor and its selection, and ⏎ still lands there. Words still being heard become text where they stand the moment you press, so what you pointed at is what is there. A drag that runs past the glass keeps selecting, and holding it above or below the text scrolls a line at a time.

The four lines the draft holds now stay still while the cursor moves inside them, by the pointer or the arrows, and scroll only when the cursor leaves them. Moving the caret ends what ⌘Z takes back, as in any Mac field: type, click somewhere else, type again, and ⌘Z undoes only the second part. And ⌘⌫ while you are dictating now takes the words still being heard along with the line, as ⌥⌫ already took them with the word.
