The walkthrough says what each tool is before it says what to press.

Every card now opens with a definition. The searcher is a list of your
apps summoned by name. The graph is a tree of letters, each ending at an
app. A breath is a saved snapshot of the windows in front of you. A
description that starts with what a tool is for leaves somebody able to
follow instructions and unable to decide when to reach for one, so each
card ends with exactly that: when this is the right tool.

Everything that is not the definition moved to a second card beside the
first. Keys and what they do, and the one line about when to use the
thing, read as secondary because they are somewhere secondary, rather
than crowding in under a definition and flattening it.

Lessons no longer take you anywhere. Pressing a gesture opens the real
panel, over a walkthrough that stays exactly where it is, and every
surface is put into a rehearsal for as long as it is up: the searcher
opens nothing, a clipboard letter pastes nothing, a hint label presses
nothing, the action menu runs nothing. Previously the walkthrough hid
itself to make room, which let macOS bring another application forward
and took the reader with it.

The name is asked for on the first card and required before it can be
left, the caret sits against the text rather than a space clear of it,
and the closing note is rewritten.
