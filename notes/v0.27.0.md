The instrument learns its own score.

Select stops charging for certainty. When the letters you have typed
match exactly one thing on screen, the pick lands without the capital:
a keystroke that could only confirm carries no information, and the
grammar no longer asks for it. The word you were midway through
typing stays understood — its remaining letters are absorbed as
confirmation, and the first letter that could not belong to it starts
the far end's search, whole. A span closes the same way, and the tail
of its end word is swallowed while the highlight stands, so nothing
you were still typing can land in the app beneath. The click door
keeps its capitals: there a pick is a click, and an action never
fires itself on uniqueness. `select.commit-on-unique` turns it off.

The coach learns two things. It now reads the failure gradient: bare
keys misfire at essentially zero and prefixed chains measurably do
not, so a chain whose starts keep failing is offered the same move a
shorten performs — to a free letter, the old address redirecting
until the new curve bends, every misfire's recovery in the price.
And its track record now buys airtime: a kind of suggestion whose
accepts demonstrably bend curves waits out shorter quiets, and one
that keeps losing waits longer. Evidence still decides what may be
offered; nothing about the gates has moved.

The guide gains a decay watch. An address unused for a month starts
earning its map back, a ramp rather than a cliff, so the scaffold
returns before the stumble instead of after it.

And the report carries the product's own score: the overhead ratio,
actual input cost against a computed floor, per channel — navigation,
select, typing, pointing — with the pointing pool ranked beside it,
app by role, as the standing answer to what the keyboard should learn
next. A channel at 1.0 has nothing left to give. Everything above it
is the backlog.
