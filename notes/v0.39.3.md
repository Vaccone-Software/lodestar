Lodestar no longer waits on other apps.

To know your windows, Lodestar asks each app about them: where a window
is, what it is called, whether it is still there. It used to ask on the
same thread that passes your keys along, for every window at launch and
again each time an app opened, closed, renamed or moved one. Brave alone
opens a small window for every tab you hover. An app that had stopped
answering made each of those questions wait, and while it waited so did
your keys, sometimes long enough for Lodestar to give up and restart.
Those questions are now asked to one side, one queue for each app, so an
app that hangs holds up only its own windows. Lodestar is also ready
sooner when it starts.

The health record is more careful about which hand did what. A move,
click or scroll is now counted to the device that made it, which the
event itself says: a trackpad marks its touches and gives its scrolls a
beginning and an end, and a mouse does neither. Before, with a mouse and
the trackpad both attached, every reach counted as the mouse's and every
smooth scroll as the trackpad's, including a mouse that scrolls smoothly.
And a day in the record now runs from four in the morning to four in the
morning on your own clock, so an evening at the keys counts as one day,
not two.

The editor also now keeps a quiet note of how long the apps you write in
take to answer it, so the next thing it gets faster at is the thing that
costs you most.

And Lodestar no longer quits when the microphone changes hands at the
wrong moment. The draft readies its microphone at launch and lets it go
when the audio devices change, and a second change arriving just after
could reach the microphone it had already let go of. It is now kept a
few minutes before it is freed. The new release check found this one:
each build is now started and put through a click, a scroll and a
keystroke by itself before it ships.
