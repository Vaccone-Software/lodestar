The coach learns to wait properly, and to keep small things to itself.

Four corrections, all of them found by reading the curriculum against a
week of what actually happened rather than against its own intentions.

A suggestion that wants a particular moment — a route wants its host, a
meetings offer wants the seconds after you join one — holds out for it
for four days and then goes out at any quiet boundary instead, so that
nothing waits forever. It was counting those four days from the moment
the app launched. Lodestar restarts on every update, and four
uninterrupted days is a long time, so on most machines that fallback had
never once opened and a suggestion whose cue was rare simply never spoke.
The clock is now kept on disk and counts real days.

There was a floor on how useful the very first suggestion had to be, and
none at all on the ones after it. Every other gate in the engine asks
whether a finding is _true_, not whether it is _worth saying_, so a
finding that saves you six seconds a week — five minutes a year — earned
the same three appearances as one that saves you three minutes a week. A
chip costs a minute of screen and a decision. Anything that cannot repay
that is now kept quiet.

A chip you ignored and a chip you never got to see used to be the same
event. Since the last release they are not: a chip that stands its whole
minute and goes unanswered is now recorded as read and passed over, and
waits four days before trying again instead of two. It still is not a
"no" — nothing is spent, and the suggestion stays on offer — but the
coach stops asking quickly about something you have already looked at.

And a meeting chip now takes the screen from a coach chip that is already
standing. Meetings have always outranked the coach; the rule simply had
no way to reach a chip that was up before the meeting was.
