The microphone comes back.

0.28.0 moved the draft's audio engine off the main thread and the
tap stopped dying, but the engine's own configuration-change
notification was answered from inside the notification, and the
notification center waits for its observer while the engine's IO
queue waits for the notification center. One Bluetooth profile flip
later the audio queue was deadlocked for the life of the process:
every draft after the first showed its input and heard nothing. The
notification now does nothing but hop, never touches the object it
carries — the center posts it for an engine being torn down too, and
holding that one crashed the first build of this fix — and the engine
restarts in place a beat later, retried through the flip's -10868
rather than abandoned on it, which is what left the older builds'
failed restarts silent. A rebuild is the fallback, not the answer:
rebuilding on every change was tried, and each fresh engine drew a
fresh change until the tap came down every second.

Three more CoreAudio reads leave the main thread with it: the call
sense that watches whether the microphone is open, the playback
pause's read of the output rate at the draft's close, and the
register line's roll call of inputs. Each waited on the HAL exactly
when the radio was flipping.

The draft now says "opening the microphone" from the door until the
recognizer reports listening, and a session that never reports is
named and stopped after eight seconds, so the line cannot say it
forever and `lode .` starts a fresh one. Insert mode answers ⌘Z and
⌘⇧Z with the editor's undo and redo of the insert run, ⌘A with the
whole buffer selected, and ⌘X with a word about what would be.

The coach prices a breath by the switches that pulled a window into
view — placed from parked, minimized, or hidden, or unparked for an
outside road — and not by every switch between the pair: a pair
already standing side by side is switched by focus alone, and the
first version promised eighteen minutes a week for a breath that
already existed. Select's band names the window it read while the
hand decides, and the record keeps whether the pointer rested on it,
the split that will decide whether select should read the window
under the pointer instead. And the last dashes are gone from surface
copy.
