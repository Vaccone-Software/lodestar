The draft now writes what you meant, not only what you said.

Say "use the red one, actually no, the blue one" and it lands "use the
blue one". A start you began again, a word said twice, a file name spoken
as "draft controller dot swift", letters spelled one by one, a list you
counted out as first, second and third: each is written the way you meant
it, once the second ear has heard the phrase again. A language model
proposes the change and a checker decides, word by word. Only words you
took back may go, and only spoken names and lists may be written as one,
never a word added and never one replaced, and a name you have written
stays exactly as written. Anything else and your words stand as heard.
On the maker's own recordings it takes the words wrong from 6.1 in a
hundred to 4.6, and to 3.9 on the largest model, and across two thousand
checked answers from seven models not one added or changed a word that
mattered. Each change is one step of ⌘Z, and a change you undo is never
offered again. There is no switch: a checker that never adds a word is
what makes one unnecessary.

Speak's models are now whole setups, chosen in Speak and nowhere else.
Standard is Parakeet with Gemma 4 E2B, from 16 GB; Full is Qwen3-ASR with
Gemma, from 24 GB; and Max, new, is Qwen3-ASR with Qwen3.6 35B, from 64 GB.
Each names both of its models in the list, and one this Mac cannot run
says how much memory it needs. A Mac with 8 GB dictates with Apple's
recognizer and the draft's own pipeline, and Automatic says so. What Write
runs never decides what Speak runs; where the two use the same model they
share one copy, on disk and in memory. Automatic on a Mac with 64 GB picks
Max, which fetches about 20 GB the first time you dictate unless Write
already holds the same model, and never over a metered connection.

In Settings, a search's border now stays only until your next key or
click, and only on the row it found: it no longer lingers on a field, or
lights the same row in the next place you open.
