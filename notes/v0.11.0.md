Lodestar introduces itself now.

The thing that makes it safe to install is also what makes it easy to
miss. Nothing here moves your windows, remaps a key, or has an opinion
about your setup until you ask it to, so a new copy sits there changing
nothing at all, including for somebody who does not yet know what to
press. So there is a walkthrough, and its job is to hand over the first
gesture and then get out of the way, because the app already teaches
itself after that through guides, chips and the cheat sheet.

Four questions, one screen each, in the order you actually meet them.
Where do you want to be, which is the searcher and the graph. What about
once you are there, which is click hints and scroll. What did you copy.
And where on the web, which is also where the question of letting
Lodestar route your clicked links belongs, since by then you have watched
a row wear the profile it will open in.

Every lesson is performed rather than described. Pressing lode space
opens a searcher inside the walkthrough that filters your real
applications, with their real icons and their real addresses. The hints
lesson labels the walkthrough's own controls, because a modal has nothing
else on screen to label. The clipboard lesson gives you two things to
copy and then pastes one back. Nothing gated: return always moves on, so
a lesson that does not register cannot trap you.

If your graph is empty, the first screen offers one, drafted from the
applications you have open, since what is running while somebody installs
a window manager is their working set. It is a proposal and never a
write: one keystroke takes it, backspace drops a line, and skipping it
costs nothing. Where a graph already exists nothing is proposed at all.

The permission grant gets a screen of its own, and advances itself the
moment you allow it, because that is the one step where the waiting is
not yours to sit through twice.

It is modal, over a blurred and darkened desktop with a sky behind it, and
it is made of the same glass as every other surface in the app, at the
same radius, so that what your hand learns there transfers without
translation. Left arrow goes back from anywhere. Escape leaves at any
point, and the menu bar has it under How Lodestar Works whenever you want
it again.

The last screen says why any of this exists, which is a personal letter
and the only place in the app that speaks in the first person.
