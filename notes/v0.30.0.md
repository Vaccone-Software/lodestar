Every input pastes.

The launcher, the commands bar and Ask are real text fields, and none
of them would take a paste. AppKit routes the editing chords through
the main menu's key equivalents, and a menu-bar app has no main menu
until it makes one; Lodestar makes one now, never shown, carrying
only the chords a text field answers, so ⌘V, ⌘C, ⌘X, ⌘A and ⌘Z land
in those fields the way they land everywhere else on the Mac. The
surfaces that read their keys off the tap learned the same key on
their own terms: in the strip's search band ⌘V folds the pasteboard's
text to one line and adds it to the query, with the strip open and no
band yet it opens one with that text, and in select and the click
door it becomes the search, so an identifier pasted lands on a
settled world exactly as one typed would.

Images are found by what they show. A screenshot on the strip could
only be found by scrolling; now the pixel sensor reads it once, off
the main thread, a beat after the card exists, and the text under the
size line is what the search reads. A screenshot of an error message
is found by the error. The screenshots already in the history learn
their captions once, in the background, a while after the first boot.

Browser copies carry their page. Chromium leaves the page's address
beside every copy, and the card now wears its host beside the app
icon and searches by it, because "the one from GitHub" is how a hand
remembers a clip that never says so.

A restore is not a copy. A clipboard manager putting the previous
contents back after a paste of its own marks them; the card is
already in the history, and promoting it reordered the list for
something the hand never copied. It stays where it was now, and a
restore of something the history never saw is still recorded.

The strip records itself the way every other surface does: how the
card was addressed, what the search cost, the hesitation before the
first key, how it ended, never the clip. Pasting from it is a channel
in the overhead ratio, against the two keystrokes it should cost, and
the strip's own open time joins the latency line.

Smaller: the pin column draws through the highest slot in use and one
free slot after it, so one pin no longer stands beside four empty
cards; the pasteboard poll carries a tenth of a second of tolerance so
the system can fold its wakeups into timers it was firing anyway; and
the design document agrees with the code about five pins.
