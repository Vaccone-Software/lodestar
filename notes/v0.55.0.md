Dictation hears you sooner and gets more of it right, and the editor checks your writing in about half the time.

The draft now hears you with Nemotron, a model that runs on this Mac, in place of Apple's recognizer. Your words appear about 0.4 seconds behind your voice, where they were 1.2 seconds behind, and once they appear they are never rewritten as you go on. On the recordings it was measured on, fewer words came out wrong (8.7% against 11.4%) and more names came out right (84% against 79%). Lodestar fetches it first, before anything else dictation uses, and keeps it ready from launch, so no draft waits for it. Until it is here, when the Mac is very short of memory, or when you dictate in a language other than English, Apple's recognizer hears you as before.

When you press ⏎, the draft lands at once. It waits only for Parakeet, which takes about a tenth of a second. Qwen3-ASR then hears the last fifteen seconds again behind it, and its words go in if they arrive before the draft lands. It used to hear up to a minute again, which took several seconds on a long dictation, so ⏎ waited its full second and often landed the words unchanged. On the same recordings, 7.1% of words came out wrong and 97% of names came out right, where it was 9.5% with a one-second wait.

What dictation runs now follows your Mac's memory, and you no longer choose it. From 8 GB it runs Parakeet alone, where Apple's recognizer was all a Mac that size had before. From 16 GB it adds Qwen3-ASR and Gemma, from 24 GB the larger Qwen3-ASR, and from 64 GB the largest language model. Only the models your Mac runs stay on disk. Settings shows what is in use instead of a menu.

The editor checks a sentence in about 250 milliseconds on average, where it took about 420, and a long sentence no longer takes longer. A corrected sentence is mostly the same sentence, so the model now reads your words as its guess and writes only where it would change them. Its answers are exactly what they were.

Grammar is checked when you pause, not when you reach a full stop, so a message you send without one is checked too. It looks 0.6 seconds after your last change, where it used to notice a pause one to two seconds late, and starts with the sentence you are in. Marks hold still while you type. A mark no longer disappears at a keystroke and comes back at the pause, and a typo marked as you finished the word no longer vanishes a second later.
