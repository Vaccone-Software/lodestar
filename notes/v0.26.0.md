The draft: text in flight toward an app.

`lode .` opens it listening. Speak, and the words settle into a panel at
the foot of the screen; type, and the characters land at the same
cursor, so a spoken sentence and a typed identifier read as one line of
prose. `⏎` puts the text on the pasteboard and pastes it where your
cursor already was, and never presses the app's own enter: sending is
the next verb, and it is yours. `⇧⏎` is a new line. The panel is never
key, so the app under it keeps its cursor and its selection the whole
time.

The destination is live. A line above the text names the frontmost app
as it changes, so the draft can be opened anywhere, carried to Slack with
⌘⇥ or `lode S`, and landed there. With no field to paste into, the text
stays on the pasteboard and says so. Every exit keeps the text on the
pasteboard, so a draft is always a clip in the strip, and a draft into
an app the clipboard excludes leaves no trace.

`lode ⇧.` opens it editing: the field's selection, or the whole field,
pulled in, silent, typing at the field's own cursor, and put back at
`⏎` if the field is still frontmost. The two keys set posture inside the bar as well, and
the mic writes only in insert mode, so `esc` silences it and `i` brings
it back. Backspace takes the grain of the last input, a character after
typing and a word after speech. The recognizer's volatile words show
dimmed after the cursor until they settle. Normal mode is vim: motions,
operators, counts, text objects, find, replace, join, undo, redo, dot,
and the visual modes, a pure state machine tested without an app. Two
deviations, on purpose: `⏎` pastes in every mode, and only `y` and `p`
touch the clipboard, so a delete never displaces what you copied.

`draft.words` is a vocabulary of the names speech gets wrong. The probe
that preceded this release found Apple's better model ignores contextual
vocabulary and the worse one honors it, so the draft repairs settled
words itself: a token within a letter or two of one of yours, same first
letter, becomes it, case and all. Add words in Settings under
Interaction.

The register line names the microphone it is reading and shows its
level in the orange macOS uses for a live microphone, so a quiet input
is a fact on screen rather than a mystery. The name is a menu: "System
(Cypress)" says what the system default is right now, and any input can
be chosen there or in Settings under Interaction (`draft.input`). The
microphone glyph is a button, on or off. The clicked selection lands
while the draft is open; the session restarts on the new device.
Recognition runs on this Mac (macOS 26); the microphone is asked for at
the first `lode .` and never before, and no audio is kept. The record
holds counts per draft, the door, the ending, the destination, the
words, the waits, and never the text. `lodestar observations` has a
line for it.

The commands bar moves to `lode -`. It ran 2.6 times a day, and the
period goes to the verb that will run more.
