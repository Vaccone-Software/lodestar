Lodestar writes itself down.

The first audit of the raw record began with a question the data could
not answer: the way holds were timed had changed, and nothing in the
files said when. So every day file now carries, in its header, the
build that wrote it, the record format, the install and the devices
that were attached — and opens a fresh segment beside itself the moment
any of that would change mid-day, so a header never describes rows it
did not see. An era event lands in the record whenever the build, the
formats, the input settings, the screens or the keyboard layout differ
from the last time they were written down, and the monthly archive
keeps the set of builds it saw. A change in the data can now be told
from a change in the hand without reading a log.

The record grew, each column with the question it answers. A modifier's
own press is recorded — how long ⌘, ⇧, ⌥, ⌃ or fn was held, and how
many keys it covered — because a chord's sustained load is what the
letters' holds cannot show. The finger, one step finer than the hand,
because the finger is the unit a layout is changed in. The time from a
key to the backspace that corrects it, because how fast a slip is
noticed is a different fact from how often one is made. Beneath the
pointer's folded reaches there is now a raw record of the reaches
themselves, every report the mouse made on the way to a target, with
each press's button, its source — your hand, Lodestar's own pick, or
another program — and the device it was on, because a smoothness
measure nobody has chosen yet cannot be derived from three band sums,
and a pick is a click the hand did not make. The wheel's bursts are
split by precise deltas and by momentum, a drag against a flick. And
beside every window: what a pixel is on each screen, whether the lid
was down, which layout the keys were read through, and the settings
that scale a delta into a point. Never which key. Never a position.

Retention is two memories, each bounded by size and each saying why.
The behavioral ring — what you did — retires its oldest months once it
is over a quarter gigabyte, about two years at today's rate; its pulses
and windows leave first, for an archive no bound of the ring's touches.
The health record — how your hands did it — has a bound a decade out,
declared and measured; Lodestar will say so as it nears it, and it
never prunes a baseline on its own. The ring was already kept for a
year, not the ninety days the notes once said; age retires nothing now.

The health record is backed up. The data folder used to sit outside
Time Machine as a whole, for the right reason — the behavioral record is
browser-history-grade. Those files still stay out, one by one. The raw
presses, the raw reaches, the archived windows and the monthly rollup go
in, because they are the part nothing can recompute, and one disk must
not be the only copy of them.

The doctor prints the time to correction, the wheel by what made it,
and the clicks that were the tool's and not the hand's.
