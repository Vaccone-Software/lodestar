Every letter Lodestar lays over your work is now a key, and the words it
points at are underlined in your accent.

Labels start at J. The right hand spends its home row first, then the
top row and the bottom, each from the right hand outward, so the first
target you see is the one under your strongest finger.

Click hints, select's labels, the editor's lens and the numbers the peek
shows over your windows are all drawn as the same small key you press
everywhere else in Lodestar, solid enough to read over any page. As you
start typing a label, its key lights, so you can see the hand is on its
way before it lands.

What a key points at is underlined, never washed. The editor's mistakes
wear one full accent line, a touch heavier than before, and select's
matches wear the same line instead of a tint behind the words, with a
heavier one under the word you anchored and the span you hold. After a
scroll aim lands, the word keeps its line while scroll mode stands, so
you know which pane the wheel is in.

In the editor's lens each mistake carries one small tag above it, its
key and its fix. When two tags would cover each other, one steps up a
row and draws a fine line down to its word. A tag that did not move
draws no line at all.
