The draft no longer asks you to know Vim. It has an editing style now, in Speak, under Microphone, and a new Mac starts on Standard: the draft edits the way every Mac text field does. ⇧ and the arrows select, with ⌥ by the word and ⌘ to the line's ends, ⌘A takes everything, ⌘C, ⌘X, ⌘V and ⌘Z do what they always do, and esc closes the draft with the text kept in the clipboard. Escape used to step into Vim's normal mode, where a person who had pressed it to cancel found letters editing their text. The text is set in the system's sans, the way the rest of Lodestar is.

A selection can be spoken over in Standard too. Select a phrase, say it again, and the new words take its place in one step, so ⌘Z brings the old ones back. While a selection waits for your voice, the foot says so, and a selection longer than the four lines the draft holds opens the whole text so you can see what it will take.

Vim is still there, whole, for anyone who chooses it, and a Mac that ran Lodestar before this version keeps it without being asked. fn ⌫ deletes forward in the draft, and ⌃d, ⌃f, ⌃b, ⌃n and ⌃p work the way they do in any Mac field. Before, fn ⌫ and the Home, End and page keys slipped past an open draft or strip into the app underneath and acted there.
