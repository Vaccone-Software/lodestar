The draft is rebuilt around one idea: it should never cover the thing you
are writing about.

It holds the last four lines from every door, speaking or typing, so the
page, the terminal or the design you are describing stays in view. To read
the whole text, press `zo` in normal mode, vim's own key for opening a
fold; `zc` folds it again, and speaking folds it too, because your eyes
have gone back to the screen. Escape only changes the mode now, never the
size. A card opened from the clipboard is still shown whole, since there
you came to read.

The microphone has no button and no labels any more. Its status is the
draft's top edge: lit in your accent while it hears you, spreading from
the middle as your voice rises and curving into the corners at its
loudest; a short grey line while it is opening or has failed, with the
reason in words; no light at all only when you turned it off. Spoken words
stay grey until every pass that might change them has finished, and turn
to ink when they are final. A word the second recognizer or the clean-up
rewrote is underlined until your next key, so a change made while you were
looking elsewhere can be seen and undone. The foot says where the text
will land and which microphone hears it; choosing another opens a small
card beside the draft, Lodestar's own rather than the system's.

The bars and the draft share finer edges and a softer shadow, and the
shadow lets clicks through to whatever is beneath. In Ask a saved link
reads as its name with its address a step quieter, and the profile a
search will open in is named once at the end of the field, with a row
naming its own only when it goes somewhere else. The launcher's dot for a
running app sits beside its name.

There is one night again. Lodestone was the more unusual of the two, and
the worse colour to spend a day in, so dark mode is the warm grey, and
the Background choice is gone; a config that set it still loads.
