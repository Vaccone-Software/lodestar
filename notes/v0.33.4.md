Lodestar leaves the way it came.

Until now the only way out was a command typed into a terminal, and
dragging the app to the Trash left its login agent behind, asking the
system for a binary that was no longer there every ten seconds for as
long as the machine lived. Both doors are finished now. Uninstall sits
under Quit in the menu and shows the whole plan before it touches
anything: the browser role handed back, the permissions returned, the
alert sound removed and the alert restored to the Mac's own, the links,
the app, the login agent, and last of all the running program. Config,
breaths and the clipboard stay unless you ask for them to go, because
they are yours and they survive a reinstall. And the Trash is a real
door: the login agent now runs through a stub that becomes Lodestar when
Lodestar is there, and when it is not, takes itself and the alert sound
away and stops. An app merely moved puts both back the next time it
starts.

The alert sound is Lodestar's own, so Lodestar keeps it where Sound
settings looks. A copy is placed at every start, and replaced when a
release changes it. Whether your Mac's alert uses it stays your choice,
made where you make every other one.

The menu carries verbs now, and only a few: the walk, Settings, updates,
an issue, Quit, Uninstall. Editing the config file and opening the log
were doors nobody should be asked to open, and routing clicked links was
a switch pretending to be a verb, which is what Settings is for.

Fixed: after every update since Lodestar learned to hand itself back to
its supervisor, the short-lived instance the updater started was reading
the news of its own update and taking it with it, so the Lodestar that
stayed never knew it had updated, and never said so.
