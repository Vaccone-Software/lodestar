Every word is set on one scale, and the scale has a floor.

Nine sizes chosen one at a time, from 10 up to 23, had drifted in over
a year, and the smallest sat below what a hand that lives at a screen
should be asked to read: a card's caption at 10 point in tertiary grey
measured 2.2 to 1 against its glass, less than half of what reading
needs. There are three sizes now and one for the launcher's field.
Meta at 13 for captions, keys and footers. Body at 16 for what a row,
a card, or the draft says, the size a terminal is set to. Title at 18
for a bar's name. The settings window and the walk sit on the same
three, since an eye is one eye. Tertiary grey stays for decoration and
absence and is retired for anything read; secondary measures 6 to 1
on the panels' charcoal.

The cursor is the thing the eye hunts most. In normal mode the block
is a solid plate with the letter cut out of it, the inversion every
terminal draws, in place of a faint wash over the letter. In insert
mode the bar is the system's own insertion colour at three points.

A card says 3m ago rather than 3m, one word that makes the letter mean
a time to someone who has never seen the card, and just now under a
minute. Beside its icon it names the page it was copied on or the app
that made it. Cards grew to keep their five lines at the new size, so
a wide display shows eight recents rather than nine; the record shows
no paste past the sixth.
