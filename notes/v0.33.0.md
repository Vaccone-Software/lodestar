A letter on every tab, two notes in the draft, and a click that means
it.

First, a fact that is also an apology. Yesterday's 0.32.4 was the
previous build under a new label. Xcode 27, which arrived with macOS 27,
puts a universal build in a different folder than the one the packaging
script had always read, and the script packaged a three-day-old binary
without a word. The glass tinted toward the system's ground, which the
last note described, arrives now. The label did more than mislead: a
Lodestar that believed it was 0.32.3 saw 0.32.4 on offer, installed it,
relaunched, and was still 0.32.3, so it installed it again, every three
minutes through the night. The packaging script now refuses a binary
that does not announce the version it is labelled with, and Lodestar
refuses a release that, once applied, did not change it.

lode ⇥ puts a letter on every tab of the window, and the letter switches
to it. It is the tab as your Mac names it, so it works the same way in a
browser and in a terminal, and a window with no tabs says so. The list
of windows the key used to open is one modifier away, on lode ⇧⇥.

A pick is a click. Whatever you pick in the click door, by typing what
you see or by a letter on a chip, Lodestar now clicks it the way your
hand would, in every app alike. It used to ask the app to press the
element for you, and some apps said yes and did nothing; the Share
button in Asana was one of them. The cursor ends where the click was.

The draft has two sounds. A note rises when the microphone is live, on
the first sound it hears rather than on the engine reporting ready,
because a microphone your Mac names can deliver silence. A note falls
when the words land. Nothing on cancel. Both are a fifth apart from
Lodestar's own alert sound, which ships inside the app and can be chosen
in Sound settings. Sounds, in settings, turns the pair off.

In the web bar, a pin before a profile's name says that a pin or a rule
chose it. A bare name is Lodestar's guess.

The launcher no longer counts windows beside an app. The number counted
everything the tree called a window, pickers and toasts included, and
moved while you watched.

And a window that outlives its accessibility handle is found again.
Zoom rebuilds its tree during a meeting; the meeting window kept its
identity while Lodestar's name for it expired, and for seven minutes
nothing could reach it. Now a fresh handle for a known window takes
over, and every verb that needs the focused window asks the app afresh
when Lodestar's answer has died.
