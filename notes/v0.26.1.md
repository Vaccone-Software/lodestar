Music steps aside while the draft listens.

A Bluetooth headset has one radio, and it cannot carry music out and a
voice in at once. The moment any app opens its microphone, the whole
device drops to the hands-free profile — mono, telephone-band, a
volume the profile remembers separately — which is why a song turns
loud and smeared the instant `lode .` starts listening. No setting
prevents it, on any platform, in any app; while the mic is open there
is simply nothing worth hearing.

So the draft pauses what was playing, and presses play again when the
device is back on its music profile. There is no setting for this
because the condition is the setting: it happens only when the mic
about to be read and the output are the same Bluetooth radio, and only
when something was verifiably playing — held through two roll calls a
quarter second apart, so a keyboard-click app or a notification chime
never counts as music. The pause is a ⏯ aimed at whichever app owns
Now Playing, which makes it a toggle and makes it distrusted: the
draft checks what the key actually did, resumes only what it watched
stop, never touches a call, and undoes on the spot the one wrong thing
a toggle can do, which is to start something. Any doubt, at any step,
collapses to doing nothing.

The resume waits for the radio, not for a timer: the output's sample
rate rising back to a music profile is the signal, with three seconds
as the backstop. A new draft opened before the resume inherits the
quiet, and the music returns after the last one closes.
