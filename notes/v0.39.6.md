A secret on your clipboard no longer shows itself to a room.

The clipboard strip keeps what you copy, and that has always included
the key from a dashboard, the token in a terminal, and the password
someone sent you in a message. Opened during a screen share, the strip
showed every one of them in full to everyone watching. Now a card that
holds a secret draws its middle as a single bar and keeps its ends, so
a key reads as sk-proj, a bar, then its last four. The ends are enough
to tell one key from another, and the bar is the same width for every
secret, so it never says how long one is. Only the secret is hidden:
the rest of a command or a settings line reads as it did.

Nothing else about the clip changes. It is kept, pasted and searched
whole, so the key you copied to paste still pastes, and typing part of
it still finds it. Password managers already mark what they copy as
private, and those copies are still never kept at all. This is for
everything else.

Lodestar knows a secret by the shape its maker gives it, the prefixes
OpenAI, Anthropic, GitHub, Stripe, Slack, AWS, Google and others put on
their keys; by its label, a value after "password" or an API_KEY=; and
by the look of random text. What you mean to paste as it is stays as
it is: numbers, commit hashes, IDs, paths and links. A password made of
ordinary words is recognised only when something labels it.

And a copy from select holds only what it says. A span caught with the
space after a word, or a line with its indent, used to paste with a
stray space you then had to delete. Now every copy select makes leaves
the spaces and line breaks at its two ends behind, and a selection it
makes in an app is trimmed the same way, so what lights up is exactly
what pastes.
