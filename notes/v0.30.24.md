The wheel is aimed by a word, and the lens ends when the hand leaves the
keys.

Scroll mode always scrolled the same spot. Entering it put the pointer at
the window's center, and a wheel event goes to whatever sits under the
pointer, so the sidebar, the thread pane, the table header that scrolls
sideways were all out of reach. The obvious fix was to find the panes,
and it was tried and measured dead: a browser names none of them, not by
role, not by scrollbar, not by any flag on any container, and it clips
the geometry that might have given them away. Probing the pixels finds
them, at the price of a second of visible twitching per point, and still
misses a pane that is already at its end.

So the panes are not found. Inside scroll mode, `/` opens the same band
as `lode /`: type a few characters of a word you can see in the pane you
want, and the pick lands the pointer on that word and does nothing else.
No press, no focus, no copy. The wheel that follows goes to whatever
scrolls under it, and the app decides what that is, because that is the
question a wheel event has always asked. A word is the one address every
pane already paints, in a web view, an Electron app or a native window
alike, so the aim needs no discovery, no history and no cooperation from
the toolkit. Uniqueness commits, so `/thr` lands on the one word that
starts that way without a capital, and the letters you were still
spelling are absorbed rather than read as scroll keys. The read covers
the whole display, so the word may be in the window beside the one you
are in, and that window scrolls without taking focus, which the trackpad
cannot do without a click. Tab's pane cycle is gone; the record showed it
had never fired, and the measurement showed why.

The lens now ends the moment the hand reaches for the pointer. A click,
or a swipe of your own, closes scroll mode the way a click closes the
clipboard strip, because a mode left up under a hand that had moved on
would swallow the typing after the click. Lodestar's own scrolling is
told apart from yours, so the mode can never end itself.

`0` and `$` jump to the left and right edges, the other axis of `gg` and
`G`, for the boards and the wide tables the aim can now reach. And every
session leaves one record: how long, which keys, whether the aim was
used, and how the mode was left, so the lens is judged by its use and
not by its share of all scrolling, which the trackpad will always own.
