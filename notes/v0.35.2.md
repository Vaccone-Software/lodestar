Lodestar cannot freeze the way it did.

Two nights ago the first click after an update deadlocked the mouse
tap, the main thread queued behind it, and the system switched the key
tap off for not answering. 0.35.1 fixed the lock. This release removes
the class of bug and bounds the damage of anything like it.

No tap callback takes a lock any more. The key tap and the mouse tap
copy the few fields they need off the event and hand them to one queue
that owns everything else; there is nothing to deadlock on because
there is nothing to lock. A thread of its own pings the main thread
and, if the answer does not come back inside eight seconds, says so in
the log and aborts — a crash launchd recovers from in a second, with a
report naming every thread, where a freeze recovers from nothing. And
the update watchdog now blesses a new build only when it answers on its
control socket, not merely when it holds the pid file: a frozen build
is rolled back like one that never started.

The tests reach the real threads now. The harness runs the real health
monitor at a scratch directory in every scenario, a stress test drives
both taps at once against a deadline, and `lodestar --self-test` runs
that stress on the signed binary itself before it is notarized. The
suite also runs five times faster, four minutes to fifty seconds: every
scenario's glass stayed in the application's window list after the
scenario died, a hundred panels by the end, and every run-loop turn
paid for all of them. They are closed now, and a test proves a dead
scenario leaves nothing behind. And the ship stops after the build for
a hand: one click, one scroll, one keystroke on the bytes that ship.
