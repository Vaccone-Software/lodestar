Your next meeting is one gesture away, and it knows which browser you meant.

A small chip appears a few minutes before a meeting starts — the lead is
yours to set, five minutes by default — and stays for the whole meeting,
because joining late is a thing people do. Tap lode twice and you are in
it. Lode ⌫ dismisses this one occurrence: tomorrow's standup comes back,
today's dismissed one never does, restart or no restart. A chip you
joined through is spent by the join. Declined and all-day events never
get a chip, and when meetings overlap, the soonest one speaks first.

The join goes to the right place, which is the whole point. Zoom and
Teams open natively when the app is installed — the link is transformed
and handed to the app by name, never tossed to the system to route.
Everything else opens in a browser profile, and here the calendar gets
the deciding vote: a work meeting and a personal meeting on
meet.google.com are byte-identical as links, so the calendar the event
lives on is the only thing that can tell them apart. Map calendars to
profiles with `meetings.calendars`; an unmapped calendar falls through
to your `web.routes` and the ordinary chain. The chip always shows the
profile it will use and which rule chose it, because an inferred answer
can change tomorrow and a chosen one cannot.

The link itself is dug out of the event's URL, its location, and its
notes, in that order of trust — Google Calendar's Meet button hides its
link where macOS's calendar API never surfaces it, so the notes are not
optional.

Nothing happens until you ask. Meetings are off by default; the menu
bar's Offer Meetings shows one card and then macOS's own calendar
prompt — the permission is asked when you reach for the feature, never
at install, in keeping with how this app treats every permission.
Nothing about your events leaves your Mac, and the ledger Lodestar
keeps for itself records the provider and the deciding rule per chip,
never a title, an attendee, or a URL. The diagnostics log is the same:
where a join went is what a bug report needs, and what the meeting was
is nobody's.

The coach can now offer this feature the way it offers everything else:
from evidence. Meeting links you open and meeting apps you switch to
were already in the observation ledger, so when a steady habit of
joining by hand is visible — and only then — the offer appears, seconds
after you have just done the chore it would remove. The claim it makes
is priced, and where the price rests on an assumption, the assumption
is stated in the evidence rather than laundered into a number. Two taps
of lode accepts, which writes exactly one config line. Enabling the
feature retires the offer forever.

One more voice means the floor matters: the walk, the meeting chip, and
the coach all speak the same two-tap grammar, so they hold the floor in
that order and lode-lode can only ever mean one thing.
