The clipboard is rebuilt as Keep, laid out the way your hands already are.

Eight clips stand in one row, each directly above the key that pastes it.
The newest is on J, under your right index finger, two keys wide and a
step taller; K, L and ; follow, each a little shorter, then F, D, S and A
on the left hand, older and level. A gap at G keeps the two hands apart,
as the keyboard does. The left hand opens Keep and the right hand takes
what it wants, so the clip you reach for most is under the strongest
finger of the hand that is free. Keep opens at the height of the other
bars, not at the bottom of the screen, where a terminal keeps its prompt.

Above the left hand, on the line of the number keys, rest four
keepsakes: things you keep for good, each with a name and the start of
what it holds. The first thing you keep goes on 4, under the index
finger. ⌘ and a card's letter opens its actions, and K keeps it, with a
name taken from its first words, selected so typing replaces it; ⌘1 to
⌘4 moves it, and R renames it later. A keepsake from the old fifth place
moves to a free one.

The letter picks the card and the modifier picks the form, the same
everywhere: the letter alone pastes the text, ⇧ pastes it as copied, ⌃
pastes Lodestar's reading of it (a colour's name, a time in your zone, a
measurement converted, a column's sum), and holding a modifier shows on
every card what it would paste. / searches your clips and fills the same
eight places, best match first, with each match wearing the chord that
takes it and the best one ⏎. ⇥ narrows to the clips from one app. The
keepsakes never move while you search.

= in Keep turns it to Bring, which finds text in your other windows: the
one on your other display, the ones underneath, never the one you are
typing in. Type a word you saw and every line that holds it comes up in
the same places, most recently used app first; ⏎ brings the best match
to where you were typing, ⌥ and a letter any other, and ⇧ the whole line
instead of the word. Nothing is copied and nothing is kept: your
clipboard is left as it was. Password managers are never read, and
neither is any app you excluded from the clipboard.

A secret pasted back from Keep stays on this Mac and is marked so other
clipboard tools look away, and a secret is never drawn whole, on a card
or in a keepsake's name. Every card casts its own soft shadow.

Two smaller things ride along. When you respell a word dictation misheard,
the draft learns it, and hears it right the next time. And every key
Lodestar draws is now the same keycap, with Settings and the other rooms
drawing their own buttons, fields and menus rather than the system's.
