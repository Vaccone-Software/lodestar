Keep now holds files as files: a copy from the Finder becomes a card with a real picture of each file, pastes as the files themselves, follows them when they move, and leaves when they are gone.

A copy made in the Finder becomes a card of its own kind. One file shows its picture (a PDF's first page, a film's poster frame, an image itself, the Finder's icon for anything else) with its name, its folder and one line more: pages, pixels or minutes. Several files stand as a stack of pictures with their count beside the key, and their names where the card has room. The pictures are made when you copy, so a card keeps its look whatever happens to the files.

The letter pastes exactly what the Finder's own copy would, so every app takes it the way it already knows: the Finder copies the files in, Mail and Slack attach them, a terminal does what it does with a file. Before this, a card of files pasted only the file's name as text, and the Finder made a text clipping of it. `⌃` and the letter pastes where the files are as text, each path whole and in single quotes, ready for a shell. While `⌃` is held, the card shows those places.

`⌘` and the letter open what a file can do. `F` shows the files in the Finder, `O` opens one file, and `E` views one file large: a picture across the display, a PDF with its pages stacked and scrolling, anything else as the Mac previews it. With the Finder in front, `M` moves the files into the folder it shows, as the Finder's own Move Item Here, so the Finder asks about a name that is already there and `⌘Z` undoes it.

A card finds its files by bookmark, so it follows a file you move or rename, and keeps finding it after a restart. A file in the Trash does not paste (the Finder refuses it), and the pill says it is in the Trash. A card whose files are all in the Trash or gone leaves Keep, and a group of files loses the ones that are gone and keeps its place. A keepsake is never changed, and a file on a disk that is not plugged in keeps its card. Nothing leaves while Keep is open. The first look over your cards comes a few minutes after this version starts, so cards of files you deleted long ago may leave then.

The search finds a card of files by its name and extension, its folder, its kind (pdf, photo, video, folder), the site a download came from and a PDF's title, from what was kept when you copied. It never reads a file.

With `select.copy-on-complete` on, Escape after the first anchor now copies that word, as `⌘C` does. With it off, Escape only leaves, as before.

A copy from select is trimmed at its ends however it is made. Before, when the app made the copy itself, its rich form could keep a space or a blank line at the start, and a rich editor pasted it. The highlight is trimmed the same way, so it never runs over the spaces at a span's ends.

Click hints narrow more quietly. As you type a hint, the letters you have typed dim on its key, the hints those letters cannot complete are gone, and a letter no hint continues brings them all back.

The lit `⏎` keys follow your accent. Switching to System no longer leaves them in International Orange beside letters that changed.
