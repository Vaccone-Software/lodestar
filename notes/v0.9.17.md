Clipboard search stops needing you to be exact.

Searching the history used to mean typing a substring and remembering it
precisely. It now ranks. A literal hit scores highest, and the earlier
those words sit in a clip the more that clip is about them. Failing a
literal hit, the letters in order: this is what lets gitcom find the git
commit you copied an hour ago.

The two are scored apart rather than mixed, because they mean different
things. Letters in order is a permissive way to match long text, so it
sits below anything you actually typed rather than competing with it. It
also asks for two characters, since a single letter appears in order in
very nearly every clip ever copied, which is noise rather than a search.

Ties keep the newest first, so a searched strip labels itself the same
way an unsearched one does and your hand reaches for the same key.

None of it costs anything at typing speed. A full history, ten thousand
clips of two thousand characters each, is searched in about forty
milliseconds, and that ceiling is now a test rather than a hope.

The repository also gains one investigative command. lodestar probe
selection asks every open app two questions: can a range of characters
be located on screen, and can the selection be set. Those two facts
decide whether Lodestar could ever let you pick a passage by keyboard
the way it lets you pick a window. The answer is not in yet.
