Lodestar holds its place when the Mac is busy. The way it starts at login never told macOS what kind of program it is, so macOS ran it as a background job: throttled, with every thread below the priority of an ordinary program, including the one that hears your keys. It now starts as an interactive program, and that thread goes ahead of ordinary work. On a Mac under heavy load, a fixed piece of work on a test program started the same way took 0.9 s where it had taken 6.1 s. Lodestar always came back after a crash, but on a badly overloaded Mac the restart itself could starve, which once left the keys dead for 87 seconds. This should shorten that, though it has not yet been measured under that much load.

Lodestar restarts one extra time right after this update, so the change takes effect at once and not at your next login.

An install can no longer pass for an uninstall. When Lodestar's login item found the app missing, it took that to mean the app had gone to the Trash and removed itself, so a crash at the moment an install was replacing the app could leave Lodestar with no login item. It now waits two minutes for the app to appear, and starts it as soon as it does. Dragging the app to the Trash still cleans up after itself, only a little later. Lodestar's own updater now exchanges the old and new builds in one step, so there is no moment with no app in place.

Moving the app to another Applications folder no longer costs the login item. The login item started from the old location, found nothing there, and removed itself. It now starts from the new one.

The log names each step of launch. A launch that ran slowly used to leave no trace of which step held it. Now a slow step is written down as it ends, a finished launch writes every step on one line, and if the app gives up on a stalled launch, the log says which step it was in.
