The note saying where an address went now stops once you have moved on.

When you accept a suggestion, the old address tells you where it went
instead of reporting itself missing. Shipped last release, that message
never stopped. It was true forever, which is not the same as being worth
saying forever: its whole job is getting your hand through the switch,
and once your hand is through, a permanent signpost keeps a retired
address meaningful when the point was for it to stop being a place you
think about at all. Read a year later, long after you have forgotten
agreeing to anything, it is not help. It is archaeology.

So it ends when the switch does. The measure is your hand rather than the
calendar: once the new address has been typed enough times to have been
learned, the old one goes quiet and behaves like any address you never
bound. That paces itself. Something you reach for constantly is through
in an afternoon; something you reach for twice a week keeps its note for
as long as it takes.

There is a nine week backstop underneath, for an address used so rarely
that it would otherwise carry the note indefinitely. If a change has not
taken by then, a line of text was never going to be what made it take.
