The clipboard reads what you copied, and finds it faster.

A clip that is only a color, a moment, a measurement or a sum now
carries a note from Lodestar at the foot of its card, in Lodestar's own
serif. The clip itself stays exactly where every card keeps its text,
and it is still the only thing that pastes; the note is Lodestar
telling you what it is.

A color is shown and named: #FF4F00 is International Orange, #FF0000
is Fire Engine Red, Apple's green is Shamrock Green. The names are the
ones more than two hundred thousand people gave colors on their own
screens, so a name sits where people put it. Search finds a color by
its name, so orange finds the orange you copied as a hex.

A timestamp says how long ago it was, as a person says it: 6 hours
ago, yesterday morning, Tuesday afternoon, August 2025. Beneath it is
your clock, UTC, and any zones you keep, which you add on the Clipboard
page as a city. A zone that would only repeat the value is left out.

A measurement in the other system is read into yours, in the unit you
would reach for: 5 km is 3.11 mi, 180 cm is 5′ 11″, 72°F is 22°C. One
already in your units is left as it is. Imperial or Metric is the one
new setting, under Advanced, and it starts as your region's.

Arithmetic is read as its answer: 1234 * 1.08 is 1,332.72. A phone
number, a date, an ID or a range is never mistaken for a sum.

The search itself was the lag you felt while typing into the strip. It
had been reading every clip afresh on every key; it now reads each one
once, and a keystroke over a full history takes about a millisecond
where it took a tenth of a second, finding exactly what it found before.

The editor now works inside the draft too, with ]s and [s to move
between marks and z= and zg to fix or keep a word, and it learns which
English you write from your Mac.
