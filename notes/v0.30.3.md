A card opens in the draft.

A quarter of the text cards in a year's history run past the five
lines a card draws, and one in ten past the preview cap entirely, so
the card you wanted was often the card you could not read. Press E in
a card's actions and its whole text opens in the draft, standing above
the strip with the card lit beneath it, silent, the cursor at the top.
The panel grows to the display's visible height and widens only when
the longest line asks, so prose stays at reading width and code gets
its columns. There is no microphone in this door, and lode . inside
it says so: opening one flips a Bluetooth headset to its telephone
profile and pauses music, and a stray press over something being read
must never do that.

Two ways back, both to the strip, and neither touches the pasteboard.
Return saves changed text to the card in place, same position, same
pin slot, same age. Escape keeps changed text as a new card and leaves
the original alone, because an edit lost to a reflexive escape is the
worse failure. Unchanged text writes nothing either way. The strip
stays the only place a paste happens, so reading a card large and
then pasting it is shift command V, command A, E, return, A.

Cards say when they are long. A card that holds more than it shows
carries a count beside its age, lines for prose and code, characters
for the one long line a link is, so the hand knows a card is worth
opening before it opens it. A card that shows all of itself stays
bare.

The search is measured. At every exit the strip records how many
cards answered the last query, how many recents were on show, how
many exist, and how deep a card pasted from the search sat, never the
query. The record showed search typed twice and abandoned both times,
and nothing in it said whether search fails to find or fails to show.
