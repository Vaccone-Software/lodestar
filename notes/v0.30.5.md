One glass.

Every panel, card, and chip is now the launcher's backdrop: regular
liquid glass with the equalizer scrim inside it, differing only in the
veil's weight where state asks, a lit card or an empty slot, never in
material. The clipboard's cards and the hint chips wore a second
recipe, clear glass under a heavier scrim. Clear glass has no frost,
so a card's opacity was entirely the scrim's, and the scrim read the
material's tone once, on a cold panel before the material had sensed
what was behind it. That is why the clipboard opened see-through the
first time in a session and right every time after: the second
opening rebuilt its cards over a panel that had been on screen. The
scrim now re-reads whenever the material's tone changes, and frost
carries the opacity anyway, so a wrong first guess is a shade rather
than a hole. The chips lose the halo around their letters, which
existed to survive what clear glass let through.

The disk image's labels read. Finder sets an icon's name in the
system's label colour whatever the picture behind it, black on this
ground, and no view option says otherwise. Two pale plates under the
two names, drawn into the background exactly where Finder puts them.
