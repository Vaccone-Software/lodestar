Lodestar now tells you when to get up, and looks as it should in daylight.

After thirty minutes of work with no break of two minutes, a walk is due.
Lodestar waits for the next natural stopping point, the moment you leave
an app you have been working in or close the draft, because a word
between thoughts is taken and one mid-sentence is not. It never comes
while you are reading. The note says the fact and then what to do,
"Thirty minutes without a break" over "Walk for two minutes", with the
mark lit as far into the hour as the stretch has run: half at thirty,
whole at sixty. Sixty is the last it says about that stretch. It asks
for nothing and goes at your next key. The number comes from the
research on breaking up sitting, which finds two minutes of light
walking every twenty to thirty minutes does what standing does not.
`coach.stand` turns it off and `coach.stand-after` moves the thirty.

Light mode was the night turned over, and it read that way. It is now
made for the light. Every surface follows the Mac when it changes from
night to day, without a relaunch. Keys are white with a fine edge and a
darker lip, so a key is still a small object rather than a dark hole.
International Orange takes one deeper shade by day, dark enough to read
as text and to carry white on a lit key. And the pictures in Settings
and on the first launch are made in sand, a warm clay a step deeper
than the page. On the Settings overview the mark is the light: every
place casts its shadow straight away from the star, and a faint warm
glow spreads out from it under the ring.

The health record is described as it is. It never stores which key you
pressed, but which hand, which finger and what kind of key are enough to
partly reconstruct what was typed, the way a phone keypad's groups are.
That is why it stays on this Mac, why anything leaving it is a separate
step you agree to after seeing it, and why the guide and Settings now
say so plainly.

The record also says when it could not see: while a password field held
secure input, while the keyboard tap was off or accessibility was gone,
while health was switched off, while Lodestar was not running, and while
the Mac slept. A quiet stretch is no longer mistaken for rest, and
`lodestar observations` shows how long it was blind, and why. Each
keyboard's report rate is written down beside it, how long a key is held
is now measured from the keyboard's own timestamps whatever the clock
does, and Exact Keyboard, under Health in Settings and off unless you
turn it on, charges each press to the keyboard that sent it. It needs
Input Monitoring, and Lodestar asks for it only then.
