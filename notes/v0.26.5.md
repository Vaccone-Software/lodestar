Finds cross the draft.

The draft’s smallest navigation command used to end at a line break.
That made a multi-line thought feel like several little documents: `f`,
`F`, `t`, and `T` could name a character only while it happened to live
on the cursor’s line.

They now read the draft as one document. Every find motion crosses line
breaks without wrapping, so it composes naturally with `d`, `c`, visual
mode, and the `;` / `,` repeats. `t` and `T` still stop beside the
character you named; when a line break lies there, they choose the first
visible place on that side instead of leaving a normal-mode cursor on a
newline.

The character named by a successful find is washed in your macOS accent
color. It stays there until the next navigation or edit, so a long jump
has an answer in the text rather than a momentary flash you have to
catch.
