Lodestar has a fourth look, and it starts with the ground and the bars.

In dark mode you choose the night every surface stands on, under Accent
in General. Default is a warm grey the colour of wet clay; Lodestone is
the cold blue-grey of the stone the first compasses were made from. Both
are here because the difference is one you have to live with to judge,
and one will go in a later version once it has been lived with. Light
mode is clay, the same material as the pictures on the doors and in
Settings, and International Orange is itself at night and the same hue a
step deeper on clay, so it stays readable without becoming a different
colour.

The launcher, Ask and the commands bar choose a row a new way. Instead of
filling it with orange, the chosen row rises a step and its keys light:
the letters that reach that target without the launcher, and return. The
light lands where your hand goes next. Each key is now one small object
with a lit top edge and a shadowed lip, lit in the accent the way the
mark's faces are. The magnifying glass is gone: each bar shows what it
holds instead, windows for the launcher, a link for Ask, the menu bar for
the commands, and the caret is the one orange in the field.

The update gate also counts the pointer now, so an update never arrives
while you are reading with the mouse, or minutes after Lodestar starts.
