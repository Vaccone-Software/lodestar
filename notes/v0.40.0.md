Dictation hears you twice now, and writes what you meant.

The draft still shows your words the moment you say them. Behind them, a
second ear listens to everything you have said since you last touched a
key, and while you keep talking it writes that stretch again, better, in
place. On the voice it was measured on, the names you use every day came
out right 97 times in a hundred, where they had come out right 32, and
the words that needed fixing went from one in six to one in sixteen. The
second ear runs on this Mac. Settings has it as Second ear: Automatic
fetches the one your Mac suits the first time you dictate, the larger
for Macs with 24 GB and more, a smaller one for 8 GB, and nothing for a
Mac that would rather not. Whatever it changes is one undo away, and ⏎
waits for it at most a second.

A pause is thinking, not the end of a sentence. Stopping to find a word
used to leave an ellipsis, or a full stop and a capital in the middle of
what you were saying, and starting to type while you talked did the
same. Now a sentence ends where its words end.

Your words are written the way you write them. Lodestar used to repair a
listed name only when the recognizer's spelling came within a letter or
two of it, which is why it turned "ghostly" into Ghostty and never
turned "load star" into Lodestar. It now listens for how a name sounds:
"load star", "super base", "cloud code" and "can Dora" become Lodestar,
Supabase, Claude Code and Kindora, and an everyday word you meant stays
the word you said. With a terminal or an editor in front, the files,
functions and branches of the project it is open in are names too:
"draft controller dot swift" lands as DraftController.swift, and "local
slash dev" as local/dev. A name that does not exist yet takes the keys a
vim hand already knows, crp for PascalCase, crc for camelCase, crs for
snake_case. Spoken numbers become digits when they are versions or
counts, "um" goes, and "for Monday, no wait, I mean Tuesday" lands as
Tuesday.

The editor's model menu leads with Automatic too, and names the model it
picks. And the app now lists the open work it is built on, with the
licenses that ask to be named, in Acknowledgements.txt inside it.
