Dictation and the hand, in the order they happened.

The recognizer runs a few seconds behind your voice, so typing while
words are still being worked out is the ordinary case, not an edge one.
Three things were wrong with it.

Taking a word off something you had just said brought the whole thing
back. The words would go, correctly, and then the recognizer would
finish and paste the entire utterance in again on top of the edit. For
an unbroken dictation that is the whole session. A result whose words
you have already edited is now discarded, which is what it always
claimed to do.

Words spoken before you started typing could land after what you typed.
The reservation that keeps the order only existed once the recognizer
had shown something, and it shows late. Now a key pressed while the
microphone is hearing your voice reserves the place too, so what is
still being worked out goes in ahead of what you typed. Speak, type
while it catches up, speak again: it comes out in that order.

And a typed word ran into the spoken one before it. Say a sentence, type
the next word, and you would get `columnand`. Typing now joins by the
same rule speech has always used, which also keeps punctuation attached
and does not double a space you typed yourself.

Also: the keys behind `lode ?` no longer come back on their own. A draft
closed with them open used to reopen showing them.
