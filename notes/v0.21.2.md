Meetings, hardened by a night of looking for trouble.

The link sniffer stopped trusting declaration order. Notes that read
"moved off Zoom, join here" often keep the old link too, and the
sniffer used to pick by provider rather than by position — so the
abandoned Zoom link could beat the Meet link the organizer actually
meant. Within a field, the earliest link now wins, whoever made it.

Three link families widened: Google Meet lookup links (the named kind
schools and teams use), Zoom for Government hosts — recognized as
meetings, but deliberately joined in the browser rather than misfired
into the consumer Zoom app — and Teams classic, which now joins
natively alongside the new client, whichever one the machine has.

A Zoom password with encoded bytes survives the native transform
verbatim. The parser used to decode it and re-embed the decoded form,
which corrupts exactly the passwords that needed encoding.

The calendar fetch left the main thread. The event tap shares the main
run loop, and a calendar database is the kind of neighbor that answers
slowly on the wrong morning; no keystroke should ever wait on it.
Change notifications now coalesce instead of stampeding, and one fetch
runs at a time.

The grant walks in on its own. If calendar access is denied and later
allowed in System Settings, meetings notice within seconds and start —
the same no-relaunch promise the Accessibility grant has always kept.
And `lodestar check` now says out loud when meetings are enabled but
calendar access is denied, which is precisely the silent state 0.21.0
shipped in: intent lives in the config, authorization in the machine,
and the doctor's job is to notice when they disagree.
