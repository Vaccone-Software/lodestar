Settings is a new room, and everything in it has a key.

It opens on ten places set around the mark, each with its picture in the
same clay as the doors and one line saying what it holds. The four doors
come first, on the keys under your fingers: 1 Write, 2 Switch, 3 Keep, 4
Speak, then Operate, Web, Meetings, Keys and Observations, with General on 0. A digit opens a place from anywhere. A place keeps its picture and its
sentence on the left and its settings on the right, under a heading for
every group, and every setting wears a letter that never moves. A place
with one switch for all of it keeps that switch on a. What you have set
wears a dot and a way back; what Lodestar chose says so, Automatic or
System, with what it picked. A permission says whether it is granted and
opens the pane of System Settings that grants it, and a place that needs
you wears a dot beside its name. lode , opens the place for whatever is in
front of you: over the strip, Keep; over the draft, Speak; over the
editor's marks, Write; over Ask, Web.

Search finds a setting by its name or by the word you would use for it,
clipboard for Keep or dictation for Speak, shows the two keys that reach
it, and lights the row it lands on with a thin border in your accent. On a
Mac with Apple Intelligence you can ask in your own words, make scrolling
slower, and Apple's model on the Mac suggests the row. ⌘Z undoes the last
change you made there, and History lists every change to the config, from
Settings, the file, the coach or the editor, each with its undo.

What Lodestar observes is now two records with two switches. The logbook
is how you move between apps and windows, and the coach reads it; health
is the rhythm of your hands, when each key goes down and how long it is
held, by hand and finger, never which key. Either can be off with the
other on, each has a size limit you can set, and either can be deleted on
its own, in Settings or with lodestar observations clear and --logbook or
--health. With the logbook off the coach rests and nothing is written,
from the moment the app starts. The first launch moves the health you have
already kept into its own file, once.
