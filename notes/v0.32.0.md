The draft stops explaining itself, and starts listening better.

The draft was the last surface carrying a legend. Three keys sat under
your words on every opening, fading on a timer, and they were the three
that fit rather than the three that mattered. Now the draft shows
nothing and answers `lode ?` with its own keys, the way every bar
already does.

Which leaves room to say something worth reading. The keys open with
the mode you are in and one plain line about it, and the editor's own
panel teaches the grammar rather than listing the alphabet: the verbs
stand alone, the group says what follows them, and the wrapping verbs
that are not vim at all finally have somewhere to be found. Nobody
guesses `sa`.

One undo used to take back a whole dictation. Speech was joining the
typing run, and a dictation never leaves insert mode, so the run was the
entire session: eighty-seven words on one keystroke. Each thing the
recognizer settles is its own step now.

And speaking over a selection replaces it. Select a phrase, say it
differently, and the words take its place. One undo brings the selection
back exactly as it stood, which is what makes it worth trying.

The microphone can no longer hang. Opening it waits on a queue that
CoreAudio can block, and a queue wedged by a device changing hands after
a restart never came back: the draft would say it was opening the
microphone until it was closed and opened again. Every attempt is
bounded now, and the watch that was meant to catch this was waiting
eight seconds, which is longer than anyone waits.

The register line found one line, too. Everything on it shared a centre
and still looked crooked, for three reasons measurement found and the
eye only felt.
