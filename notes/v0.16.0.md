Lodestar follows your appearance: light mode arrives.

Every surface — the launcher, the guides, the cards, the clipboard
strip, the walkthrough, the hint chips — now takes its tone from your
system setting. Dark mode keeps the charcoal instrument it has always
been. Light mode gets the same instrument in paper and ink: light frost,
dark type, the same accent. The walkthrough's night sky becomes a star
chart.

One thing deliberately did not survive: the glass no longer decides its
own tone from whatever sits behind it. Liquid Glass adapts per panel to
the backdrop, which is how a panel could turn light over a bright page
while its text stayed dressed for dark — the invisible menu a beta
user found in his first ten minutes. Now the material still frosts, and
what glows through is still the world behind it, but the tone is yours:
one look per setting, identical on every summon, over any backdrop. A
surface you read in under a second should never cost a reorientation.

The walkthrough also learned to keep its hands to itself. It used to
listen for its keys even while you were off in System Settings granting
the permission it asked about — on the wrong screen at the wrong moment
that could swallow the keyboard entirely. It now listens only while its
own deck is focused, and lets every key pass otherwise.
