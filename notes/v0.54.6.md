The pictures in Settings and on the first-launch doors are cameos now. Each one is the old clay model of its door or place, turned to face you, pressed into a low relief and lit by one light from above, the way Ceramic lights everything. The letters are inlaid in ink and the orange is a glaze, so a picture reads as something made rather than something rendered, and it sits on its slab without a shadow of its own.

Hints wear capitals. In select, the click door and the aim band, a lowercase letter searches and a hint is picked with Shift, so the hint now shows the key you actually press. An I and an l no longer look alike, and a hand that reached for the lowercase letter out of habit has a capital in front of it to correct it.

A search field holds only its words. The / that sat inside the Settings search and the clip bars is gone, along with the = in Bring, and the words start where the field starts. Where / is still shown as a key, it is drawn at the letters' size instead of as a speck.

Mouse scrolling is retired. A mouse's own app or a scrolling utility turns the wheel better than Lodestar did, and scroll mode keeps the direction fix from 0.54.2.

Lodestar freezes less when the Mac is short of memory. The editor asked macOS every second which app was in front, and the answer came from another process on the thread that draws everything and reads your keys. Under memory pressure one answer could take a second or more. Lodestar now hears when the app in front changes and remembers it, so that thread no longer waits.
