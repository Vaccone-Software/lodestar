Dictation gets the better ear far more often. The second ear that hears each phrase again used to let go of its memory five minutes after the draft closed, so more than a third of drafts started without it, and it then took ten seconds to load because it loaded at the same moment as the much larger model that writes what you meant. Now it stays for thirty minutes, loads first, and leaves only when the Mac runs critically short of memory. When it does finish loading partway through a draft, it hears again the words you said while it was loading, from the audio the draft still holds, instead of skipping them.

⏎ lands sooner. It used to wait for every hearing the ear had queued, most of them already out of date, and for a rewrite that rarely finished in time. Now the ear runs one hearing at a time with only the newest waiting, and ⏎ waits for a rewrite only when its own record says it can finish within the wait. While it does wait, the foot says "finishing", and a second ⏎ lands at once with what is there.

Fewer words go missing. A final for words you had just edited used to be dropped whole, taking with it anything you said after them. Now your edit stands and the rest of what you said lands after it. ⏎ waits for the recognizer's last results to arrive rather than landing on the first one. Esc still closes at once, but the clipboard now waits a moment for the last words you said. A draft cut off by a crash, a force quit or a power loss comes back at the next launch as a card in the clipboard history, without replacing what you copied last.

⏎ no longer pastes into thin air. When the app in front has nothing focused that can take text, such as Finder with a folder selected, the draft copies the text instead, says so, and plays no landing sound. Terminals, browsers and anything uncertain paste as before.

Editing works the way a Mac field does. A dictated paragraph is one long line, so ⌘⌫ used to delete the whole paragraph and ↑ jumped to its first character. Now ↑, ↓, ⌘←, ⌘→, ⌃A, ⌃E, ⌃N, ⌃P and ⌘⌫ follow the lines you see, in both editing styles. ⌘Z after you edit what you just said takes back the edit and leaves the sentence. ⌥ types what it types everywhere else, including é, the em dash and curly quotes, with dead keys. A word after a closing quote no longer runs into it.

The microphone is more patient with Bluetooth headsets. A headset switching to its calling mode changes its sample rate, and starting the microphone during that switch failed or produced an engine that heard nothing, which cost several seconds before the headset took over from the Mac's own microphone. Now the draft waits for the rate to settle before it starts again. If the microphone was refused permission, lode . opens Privacy & Security at Microphone, and a microphone that is refused, failed or hearing nothing is reported on the foot before any other note.

A pause after "her" or "in" no longer swallows the period when the next sentence starts with "she" or "but", and fewer ordinary phrases are sent to the model that writes what you meant, so it is free sooner for the phrases that need it.

The editor marks less, and what it marks is worth the look. It used to underline a word whenever the Mac's spell checker would have fixed its casing or its apostrophe, so "jeff", "github" and "dont" each wore a line, and a lowercase name drew a stranger as its fix, "priya" offered "prayer". Your casing and your apostrophes are yours, so those words are left alone now, as are #channels, /commands, :shortcodes: and chat's own words. A word the spell checker is wrong about by more than a slip or two is no longer offered a fix it was only guessing at. Every common typo still marks.

The grammar model is held to the same rule. Read over 265 passages of the maker's own writing, more than a fifth of its marks were style rather than mistakes: a contraction written out, "it's" to "it is", a digit spelled as a word, and commas before "and", "because" or "when", or after a lone "However". Those are no longer offered, and the same passages now draw 76 marks where they drew 98. A comma that splits what belongs together, "about, the plan" or "your, notes", still is.

Launchers, search boxes and the browser's address bar are no longer read at all. In its first weeks the editor put 368 marks in Raycast and not one was fixed, because a query is not writing.

Keep as written now lasts while you write. Leaving a word alone with ⇧ and its letter quieted it only until your next keystroke in the same sentence, which is exactly where the mark nearest the caret always is. Now it stays quiet for as long as the words before it stand.

A fix in Slack, Brave, Claude and the other Chromium apps lands at once, without the moment it spent waiting on a write those apps ignore, and keys you strike while it lands are typed after it rather than over the word. A second letter in the lens right after a fix that changed the length of the text now lands on the right words. And a card no longer opens for a pointer that is only passing over the text. It waits for the pointer to rest.

The grammar model no longer stops for good. While you dictated, the editor's model and the ear that settles your speech used the GPU at the same moment and slowed each other, until the editor gave up on every sentence and stayed that way, once for fifty minutes, until Lodestar restarted. Now they take turns, a sentence that could not be asked is asked again a little later, a model that stops answering is loaded afresh, and if grammar is paused for a while the editor says so once, since a missing mark would otherwise look like a clean sentence.

The record of what the editor does is honest now. It counted a mark again each time the text before it moved and each time a field blinked out for a moment, and it counted every typo you fixed yourself as a mark ignored, which put its fix rate at 5% and said nothing true. Now each mark that stood for a second ends in one outcome, fixed by the editor, fixed by you, edited into something else, kept, or left standing when the message was sent, and `lodestar observations` shows them that way. The marks also stop blinking when an app rebuilds its field behind the same text.
