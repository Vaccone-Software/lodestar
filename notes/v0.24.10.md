Lode backspace failed sometimes, and the way it failed was worse than
not working.

Holding lode for half a second raises the graph, and every key pressed
after that takes the graph back down. Taking it down is a handover of
the glass, and a handover of the glass retires whatever chip was
standing on it — so by the time the dismissal you asked for went looking
for something to dismiss, the answer had already been cleared off the
screen. It reported that it had done nothing, and a gesture that has
done nothing is passed along to whatever is in front of you.

Lode is a command key. So the dismissal became ⌘⌫ delivered into another
app, and in the Finder ⌘⌫ is move to trash.

The chip is asked first now, before anything comes down. It keeps the
one condition it should have kept, that the keyboard is otherwise idle,
because ⌫ inside a breath chain arms that chain's deletion and inside
hints and select it edits — an idle keyboard is the only one with a
spare ⌫ to give. It has stopped asking who typed it. Agreeing to a
suggestion writes a line to your config and should be a hand's work; a
dismissal writes nothing, and refusing to dismiss under a keyboard
remapper is the worse of the two failures. And when it declines, it says
so in the log, which is the part that would have made this a five minute
question instead of a habit you worked around.

**Chips can be moved.** Drag one anywhere. It is the answer to a chip
sitting over the thing it is telling you about, and there was no answer
before. The position is deliberately not remembered: a chip returns to
its corner next time, because a drag answers this chip over this window,
and a position learned from one bad overlap would be carried to every
chip that never had the problem.

**And they can be pressed.** The keys drawn on a chip are now the
buttons too. Hover one and it lights and the pointer turns; press it and
it does what the keys do. Nothing new was added to press — the caps were
already the picture of the gesture, and they have simply stopped being
only a picture. The coach's chip grew a second row for the same reason:
its way out lived in a sentence, and a way out you cannot click is not a
way out for a hand already on the mouse.

This reaches the meeting chip, the link chip, the coach's chip, the
walk's card, and the calendar permission card. The chain guide is
deliberately left alone. It is not an offer, it is the pending state of
a gesture already in progress, and a surface your hand is mid-sentence
with should not be catching clicks or sliding around under a stray
press.
