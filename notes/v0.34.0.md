Lodestar keeps the record.

Until now the health pulse kept summaries: counts, moments, a histogram
of how long keys were held. A summary answers the questions it was
built for and no others. Beneath it there is now a raw record: every
press, when it went down and when it came up, which hand, what kind of
key, and whether a modifier, a lens, or Lodestar itself was involved.
Never which key. One file a day, compressed when the day closes, on the
order of fifty megabytes a year, in the keys folder beside the rest of
your data. Nothing in it is analyzed. It is kept so that a question
nobody has asked yet can still be put to this year's typing.

Above the record, ninety-second windows describe the typing habit the
way the clinical literature reads it: exact quantiles, the distribution
at the edges the published features use, the fluctuation between one
press and the next, the rollover when a key goes down before the last
one came up, all of it kept for each hand and each direction across the
keyboard. Each window also notes the app, the kind of element that had
focus, the keyboards attached, the power source and the screens, because
a hold time is a mechanical fact and a different keyboard is a different
instrument. The pointer's reaches keep their speed profile from the
mouse's own counts. The monthly archive keeps each window's spread as a
sample, so a month holds the spread of the spread.

Two optional facts about you live under Health in the Coach pane: the
year you were born and the hand you write with. Age and side are the
first things any reading of the hands is adjusted for. Both stay on the
machine, like everything else here.

The design note moved one line. The discipline is not in what the
instrument collects but in what it says and what leaves the machine.
Nothing leaves. What it says is still nothing: the printout describes,
and a description is not a verdict.

Fixed: every hold was timed from the moment the event tap got around to
it, on the main thread, so a key released during a window rearrangement
was measured tens of milliseconds long. Presses and releases are timed
from the event's own stamp now, on the keyboard and the mouse alike.
