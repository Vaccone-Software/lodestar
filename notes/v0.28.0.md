It looks where the hand is going.

The warmer used to wait for the switch. Chromium and Electron tear
their accessibility trees down after idle minutes, so the first hints
or select in a freshly reached browser paid for a tree that could have
been standing. Now the three apps most likely to be next are warmed
before you arrive — the app you just left is the single best guess,
a first-order chain carries the rest, and the blend's two numbers are
tuned on your own stream, then re-tuned every six hours. On the stream
this was built against, three warmings covered nine switches in ten.
The cost stays where it cannot be felt: one attribute per app, once
per half minute, after the hand settles, off the main thread, behind
a quarter-second leash, and only for apps with a live window. Every
strategy is still scored in shadow in `lodestar observations engine`,
so the choice stays checkable.

The instrument sees two more columns. Every focus change is charged
to the hand's most recent act — a Lodestar route, the system's ⌘⇥, a
click, or nothing the tap could name — so the record can say how a
switch happened, not only that it did. And the backspace is counted
in runs: a single is a typo, five in a row is a sentence being
retaken, and only the second is anyone's business to shrink.

The coach closes a road. The nudge — an address exists and the
launcher keeps winning anyway — stopped being report-only. Accept it,
and the launcher, asked for that app, says which address reaches it
and opens only on a second ↵: one deliberate confirm, the escape
hatch priced in deliberation rather than removed. The toll lifts when
the address's curve bends or at the same nine-week cutoff every
redirect obeys. And the redirect is universal now: a hand edit, a ⌘K
retarget, or a settings change moves a binding the same way an
accepted supersede does, and the old address says where it went —
unless the move is ambiguous, because a wrong signpost is worse than
a miss.

The footers fade. The launcher's, the web bar's, the commands bar's,
and the draft's legends of keys wait longer as the bar is used, come
straight back for an hour after the bar is escaped with typing in it,
and forfeit their wait with a month of disuse. A hand that knows its
keys never reads them.

And the draft no longer takes the keyboard with it. Opening it
speaking on a Bluetooth headset could freeze every key on the machine
for a second or more: the audio engine started on the thread that
hosts the event tap, and a start caught inside the headset's profile
flip fails slowly before it succeeds — three times in one day's log,
macOS disabled the tap for it. The engine now lives on its own queue,
the radio check with it, ⏎ lands within two seconds even if the
recognizer never answers, the field under the cursor is read on a
short leash, and a document's worth of paste is refused at the door.
