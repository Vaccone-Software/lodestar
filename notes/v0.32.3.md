Dictation stops opening a microphone that is not there.

The input your Mac calls the default is not always a microphone. A dock
or a monitor can present one, take the default, and deliver silence,
without anyone having chosen it. The draft follows the system, so it
opened that silence on every dictation, and kept opening it, until the
hardware happened to be unplugged.

Now a device that delivers nothing twice is set aside and your Mac's own
microphone is used instead. Being heard somewhere is better than being
silent faithfully.

The other half is that nothing used to say so. The recognizer's
preparation has no time limit of its own, and when the speech stack is
stuck it simply never answers, so the draft sat there reporting that the
microphone did not start while the thing that would have started it was
waiting forever. Every step of opening the microphone now has its own
deadline and names itself when it gives up, which is also why you will
hear about a real failure in a few seconds rather than after a long
silence.
