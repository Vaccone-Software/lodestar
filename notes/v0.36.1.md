Dictation with the lid closed, and on a headset that was slow once.

A closed lid switches your Mac's microphone off. Not quietly: every
program reading it receives exact zeros, at the full rate, and the
draft read those zeros through an evening in clamshell, sixteen
sessions with the glyph lit and nothing behind it. It had a test for a
microphone that delivers nothing, but the test counted buffers, and
the zeros arrived as buffers.

The engine is now watched for signal. A room never reads below a
whisper of noise; a dead input reads nothing at all, and nothing at
all for a second and a half, four on a Bluetooth headset whose
telephone link takes that long to come up cold, is what the draft acts
on. With the lid closed your Mac's microphone is not fallen back to at
all: a headset is read when one is connected, and when none is, the
register line says the reason in one line rather than listening to
silence.

The headset had its own trouble that evening. Its link came up more
slowly than the draft's budget for a start, so each start was retried,
and the retry stopped a session whose first sound had not arrived yet
and wrote the headset off as deaf. Twice, in ten seconds, after which
every session in the process was pinned to the microphone the lid had
switched off, whatever the register line was set to. Three things
changed. A start that timed out proves nothing about a device; only a
whole window of silence with the engine running does. A device you
name on the register line is read as named, always. And a device
written off is forgiven the moment the set of inputs changes, so a
headset that was slow once is not held against it after it
reconnects.

And when the microphone has been open for three seconds and heard
nothing, the register line says so: hearing nothing on, and the name.
The listening note's silence, for the eye that is on the draft.
