Lodestar is steadier where it was quietly uneven.

Dictation keeps its promise about time. When the microphone was slow to
start, the draft was meant to stop waiting at two and a half seconds and
try again, but it waited for the slow start to finish first, however
long that took, and then tried again anyway. Now it stops at the
deadline, and a start that is merely slow is left to finish rather than
begun a second time. On a Bluetooth headset, the Mac's own microphone
now keeps listening until the headset has actually heard you, not just
until it has answered, so the first words after a slow headset are not
lost in the gap. What the Mac's microphone hears no longer counts as the
headset hearing, so a headset that stays silent is still named as the
one hearing nothing.

The window you end on is the window Lodestar thinks you are on. Moving
quickly from one window to a new one and back could leave it holding
the new one, because the answer about the new window arrived last and
won. And a window you moved or resized while Lodestar was reading it is
now read again, so its last position is the one it keeps.

When two copies of Lodestar overlap, as they do for a moment at every
update, the log keeps every line of both instead of writing one over
the other.

Updates arrive more reliably. A check that fails on a poor connection
now tries again after a quarter of an hour and then an hour, instead of
waiting a day. An answer from GitHub that is an error, such as a rate
limit, is read as an error and not as "nothing new". And an update now
waits until you have really stepped away, counting your typing and a
presentation in progress, not only your use of Lodestar.

And what you were in the middle of is on disk sooner. A draft you are
dictating is kept safe every half second while you speak, not only when
you pause, so a draft cut short can come back. A window set aside to
the corner of the screen remembers where it came from the moment it is
moved. Quitting saves the clipboard's last copy. The editor, turned off
and on again, reads the sentences it had not reached. And a record of
your keys that was cut off mid-write is closed as it stands, rather than
leaving everything after it misread.
