One character never commits.

Commit-on-unique shipped in 0.27.0 with a hole its own arithmetic
hid. A one-character search matches standalone tokens only, and app
chrome is full of icons the recognizer reads as lone letters, an F
beside Report, an O beside Autofill. Type Software and the S was
unique on the page, so the anchor landed on an icon; then of, a
substring of the word, was unique too, and the span ran from the icon
to the word the hand was still typing. That is what selected the
column nobody asked for, and why the letters typed afterward seemed
to cancel it. Measured by typing every visible word on a live page
through the grammar, one word in five anchored wrong or ran away,
every one of them on a single character, and a two-character floor
took both to zero. It is the floor now, in both stages.

Uniqueness is asked only of a settled world. The fast recognition
pass is a sketch, a few lines short and rough around rare glyphs, and
an anchor landing on it used to pin it for the whole mode, the
accurate pass thrown away when it arrived a beat later. The sketch
now shows chips and commits nothing; the accurate pass, landing under
a search already typed, commits the anchor its next keystroke would
have, so a hand that finished typing before the sensor finished
reading is not charged a capital for it. The accessibility tree keeps
its own uniqueness only where it is the only sensor.

A capital arriving while the end word's tail is still pending is the
pick the hand had planned before uniqueness made it. Passed through,
it replaced the selection the span had just made, or typed into the
page beneath the highlight. It is swallowed once now, and the tail
ends with it.

The record counts the keystroke uniqueness turned into a pick, which
it used to skip, so a session that went wrong on two keys no longer
reads as a session with no typing, and every auto-fire writes one
line naming the search length and the world it fired on. The probe
learned select's bench: probe selectsense captures a window without
touching focus, runs both recognition passes and the stitching, and
types every visible word through the grammar, reporting what anchored
right, what anchored wrong, and what ran away.
